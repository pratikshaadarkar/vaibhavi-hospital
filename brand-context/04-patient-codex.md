## Section 1 — Fears & Desires

### Fears (what patients carry when they walk in)

**Fear 1: "Will we be sent somewhere else if something goes wrong?"**
This is Dr. Vaibhavi's own observation — the single most common tension she hears from patients:
> *"Their concern is usually, 'If any complication happens, won't we be sent somewhere else? Everything will be handled here itself, right?'"*

This fear is rooted in real experience. In the Taloja area, many hospitals have limited facilities. Patients have either personally experienced or heard of emergency transfers that went wrong, or simply cost more time and money than expected.

**Fear 2: "How much will this cost?"**
> *"Their main doubt is how much money it will cost. Usually, a relative comes first and asks how much it will cost. Then they bring the patient."*

Money anxiety is the primary filter before even meeting the doctor. In middle-class households in Taloja, an unexpected hospital bill can destabilise a family budget. The Mediclaim question is tied to this fear — will they cover it? Will the hospital handle it?

**Fear 3: "Is this doctor experienced enough?"**
Dr. Vaibhavi herself identified this as the one genuine challenge she faces as a newer hospital against older establishments:
> *"People may feel they have more experience because they've been around twenty years longer than me."*

This is an unspoken fear in the patient's mind — not about Dr. Vaibhavi specifically, but about choosing a new hospital in general.

**Fear 4: "Will the doctor actually listen to me?"**
Dr. Vaibhavi cited this as the most common patient complaint about other hospitals:
> *"One common complaint is that the doctor or nurse didn't properly listen to what they needed."*

Many women in this demographic have experienced being dismissed, rushed, or given a prescription without explanation. They carry this fear into every new doctor's appointment.

**Fear 5 (for pregnant women specifically): "Will my baby need to be taken away to another NICU?"**
Embedded in Fear 1, but specific to labour and delivery. The presence of an on-site NICU directly addresses this.

---

### Desires (what patients truly want)

**Desire 1: Everything in one place**
> *"They can feel completely safe and trust the facility... all the facilities required."*

The dream state is not just good treatment — it is the absence of running around. One building. One doctor she trusts. One receptionist who handles paperwork. Tests, scans, delivery, baby care — all here.

**Desire 2: To be told, not just treated**
> *"We listen to every problem. We try to solve all their problems. We look at things through the patient's eyes."*

Patients want to understand what is happening to their bodies. They ask: what should I eat, what should I avoid, will it be normal or C-section? The desire beneath these questions is: *tell me what to expect and make me feel prepared.*

**Desire 3: To feel like a person, not a number**
The recurring theme across the transcript is that patients want to be heard. Dr. Vaibhavi's proudest compliment: *"I explain things well, I listen to them, and I take all their problems into consideration."* Patients are not looking for a transactional encounter — they want to feel seen.

**Desire 4: A safe delivery with a healthy baby**
The ultimate desire for every pregnant patient. Everything else is a path to this outcome.

**Desire 5: Affordable care without compromising safety**
Patients want quality and affordability together — the belief that "affordable" doesn't mean "cutting corners."

## Section 2 — Trigger Moments

### What finally makes them decide to visit

**Trigger 1: Pregnancy confirmation**
The moment a woman tests positive, she (or her family) immediately begins looking for a gynaecologist. Proximity, affordability, and a female doctor are the primary filters.

**Trigger 2: A complication or symptom that can no longer be ignored**
PCOD symptoms that have become disruptive. An irregular period lasting 3+ months. Heavy bleeding. These are the moments that push women past avoidance into action.

**Trigger 3: Word-of-mouth from a trusted person**
> *"The patients we have seen so far came on their own. They saw the board and walked in."*

In the early days, walk-ins from the signboard are the primary source. Over time, the goal is for referred patients to become the dominant source.

**Trigger 4: First visit experience locks the decision**
> *"Mostly, whichever patients come to me return for their next visit as well. They don't go elsewhere. They develop trust."*

The first appointment is the real conversion moment. If Dr. Vaibhavi explains clearly and the patient feels heard, she comes back and brings others.

### Doubts right before confirming

- "Is this hospital new? Will they have everything ready?"
- "Can they handle a complication?"
- "How much will it cost?" (relative usually asks this on the patient's behalf)
- "Is the doctor experienced?" (not always said out loud)

### How Dr. Vaibhavi handles it
By being expressive and thorough. She explains until they understand. She shows them the facilities. She makes the invisible visible.

## Section 3 — Their Language

### Words and questions patients actually use

From the transcript (Dr. Vaibhavi's recounting of patient conversations):

- *"Will we have to be sent somewhere else?"*
- *"Can it be done here itself?"*
- *"How much will it cost?"*
- *"Will my delivery be normal or C-section?"*
- *"What should I eat? What should I avoid?"*
- *"If something happens to the baby, will we have to be shifted?"*
- *"Where do I go for the sonography?"*

### Compliments patients give Dr. Vaibhavi

> *"You explain things well."*
> *"You listen to us."*
> *"You take all our problems into consideration."*

These are not generic compliments — they are precise. Patients notice and name the specific thing she does differently: *she listens, she explains, she doesn't dismiss.*

### Complaints about other hospitals (what brought patients here)

From Dr. Vaibhavi's observations:
- *"The doctor didn't properly listen."*
- *"We had to go from place to place for sonography, pathology, blood tests."*
- *"The doctor just says everything is fine — doesn't explain what to eat, what to avoid."*
- *"Appointments were not managed properly."*

### Language note for copywriting
The patient's primary language is likely Marathi or Hindi. English is functional, not emotional, for this demographic. Copy should be clear English with warmth that reads like a human speaking, not a medical brochure.
