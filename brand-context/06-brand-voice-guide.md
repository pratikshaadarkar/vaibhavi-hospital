# Brand Voice Guide — Vaibhavi Hospital

---

## 1. Voice Foundation

Vaibhavi Hospital's brand voice is built on one truth from the founder herself:
> *"I am very expressive and I explain a lot. Until they understand, I keep explaining again and again."*

The voice of the brand is the voice of Dr. Vaibhavi as a doctor — warm, direct, clear, and human. It is never cold. It is never corporate. It never talks down to the patient.

---

## 2. Core Voice Attributes

| Attribute | What It Means in Practice |
|---|---|
| **Warm** | Writes like a caring woman talking to another woman, not a hospital marketing department |
| **Reassuring** | Addresses fear first. Tells patients what will happen, not just what services are available. |
| **Clear** | Short sentences. No jargon unless explained. Avoids bureaucratic language. |
| **Expressive** | Uses real emotion where appropriate — joy in delivery, empathy for pain, pride in outcomes |
| **Grounded** | No over-promise. No hype. No claims that can't be backed up. |
| **Affordable confidence** | Speaks to value without being apologetic about price. Never sounds desperate or cheap. |

---

## 3. Brand Personality

If Vaibhavi Hospital were a person, she would be:

> **The knowledgeable elder sister who is also a doctor.**

She knows exactly what you're going through. She explains things until you understand — without making you feel stupid for asking. She doesn't dismiss your worry. She tells you the truth, gently. She has seen worse than your situation, and she is calm because of it. She is on your side.

She is **not** the cold specialist who speaks in Latin terms and hands you a printout. She is **not** the overly formal corporate brand that calls you "Dear Patient." She is the woman you trust completely to handle the most important moments of your life.

---

## 4. Tone Reference — What the Website Should Feel Like

Dr. Vaibhavi's own words: *"It should feel fresh. And lively too."*

The website should not feel like a government health notice. It should feel like you've already found the right doctor before you've even called.

---

## 5. Words to Use

| Use This | Because |
|---|---|
| You / Your | Speaks directly to the patient, not at her |
| We / Our team | Humanises the hospital as people, not an institution |
| Safe | Central to patient fear — address it directly |
| Here | Reinforces the "everything in one place" message |
| Together | Signals partnership between doctor and patient |
| Explain / Tell / Guide | Reflects Dr. Vaibhavi's actual communication style |
| Care | Genuine, not performative |
| Comfortable | Relevant to delivery experience, consultations, room quality |
| Affordable | Key differentiator — use it with confidence, not apology |
| Every woman | Inclusive, speaks to the full patient spectrum |
| Ready | The hospital is prepared — for your delivery, for emergencies |
| All facilities | Directly addresses the "will I be sent elsewhere" fear |

---

## 6. Words to Avoid

| Avoid This | Why |
|---|---|
| World-class / International-class | Overpromise; rings hollow for a community hospital in Taloja |
| State-of-the-art (overused) | Cliché; replace with specifics (e.g. "equipped NICU") |
| Trusted (standalone, generic) | Dr. Vaibhavi explicitly flagged this as hollow in healthcare |
| Patient-centric (jargon) | Replace with a showing — "we listen until you understand" |
| We are committed to excellence | Empty corporate sentence |
| Highly qualified | Vague; replace with specifics (MBBS, MS Obstetrics & Gynaecology, 6+ years) |
| Kindly / Please be advised | Bureaucratic, creates distance |
| Complications (in headlines) | Triggers anxiety without resolution — bury it in body copy with a solution beside it |
| Best hospital in Navi Mumbai | Unverifiable; avoid superlatives without evidence |

## 7. Tone Shifts by Patient Situation

### A. General Patient (Routine OPD / PCOD / Menstrual Issues)
- **Tone:** Warm, matter-of-fact, slightly conversational
- **Goal:** Make her feel like this is a safe, normal place to come — not intimidating
- **Example (RIGHT):**
  > "Your body is changing. That's not a problem — that's information. At Vaibhavi Hospital, we help you understand what's happening and what to do next."
- **Example (WRONG):**
  > "We offer comprehensive gynaecological care services tailored to your individual clinical needs."

---

### B. High-Risk / Anxious Pregnant Patient
- **Tone:** Calm, reassuring, confident — without minimising her fear
- **Goal:** Tell her explicitly that we are prepared, that she does not need to go elsewhere, and that she is in capable hands
- **Example (RIGHT):**
  > "High-risk pregnancies need more attention — not more worry. Our ICU, NICU, and experienced team mean you and your baby are cared for at every step, right here."
- **Example (WRONG):**
  > "We handle all obstetric complications using advanced protocols."

---

### C. Adolescent / Young Woman
- **Tone:** Gentle, non-judgmental, slightly lighter — but still informative
- **Goal:** Remove embarrassment and anxiety. Make her feel like this doctor won't judge her.
- **Example (RIGHT):**
  > "If your periods have been irregular, painful, or confusing — you're not alone, and it's not something you have to figure out by yourself. Dr. Vaibhavi will explain everything clearly, without making you feel uncomfortable."
- **Example (WRONG):**
  > "We provide adolescent gynaecological consultation services."

---

## 8. Grammar & Style Rules

- Write in second person ("you") wherever possible
- Short paragraphs — 2–4 lines maximum
- No paragraph should start with "We offer" or "Our hospital provides" — lead with the patient, not the hospital
- Use plain English — if you have to explain a medical term, do it in the same sentence
- Emotional moments are earned — don't front-load with sentiment; let the story carry it
- One idea per sentence in headlines
- Avoid parentheses in public-facing copy — they suggest hesitation

---

## 9. Headline Formula Examples

**Problem → Reassurance:**
> "Worried about your delivery? You're in the right place."

**Specificity over vagueness:**
> "OPD 9 AM to 12 PM and 5 PM to 10 PM — plus 24/7 emergency care."

**Human voice:**
> "We explain until you understand. That's just how we work."

**Differentiation through specificity:**
> "Sonography, pathology, ICU, NICU — right here in the same building."
